## Description

**One line**

> A tactical guild-management RPG where you recruit heroes, manage their
> relationships, conquer dungeons, and pay for all of it.

**Short**

> You run an adventurers' guild. You recruit the heroes, assign the contracts,
> fund the buildings and settle the arguments. The heroes go into the dungeons
> and make their own decisions once they are down there — for better, and
> frequently for worse. They also form friendships, feuds and romances, take up
> hobbies on their days off, and remember what you did the last time it went
> badly. Death is permanent, and so is the grudge.

**Long**

> Hero's Guild is a management game about the people you manage.
>
> Every hero arrives with a life already in progress: a background, a family, a
> purse, a set of opinions and a position on what a guild ought to be for. They
> form bonds with each other over repeated near-death experiences — best
> friends, rivals, lovers, blood feuds — and those bonds have consequences you
> cannot overrule. Two heroes in a blood feud will refuse to go on the same
> contract, and no amount of money will change their minds.
>
> They notice things. They notice when the guild's values drift from their own.
> They notice who got the private room and who is still in the barracks. They
> notice when a friend does not come back, and they grieve in proportion to how
> close they were. On their idle days they discover hobbies, and the hobbies
> become clubs, and the clubs want a room, a budget and a weekday evening — and
> the choir annoys everybody on the floor below until it gets good enough not to.
>
> Meanwhile there is a guild to run: facilities to build, wages to pay, a
> warehouse that fills up, crafting disciplines to level, procedurally generated
> dungeons to clear, and endgame content that will take the roster apart if you
> bring the wrong five people. Combat resolves on initiative and threat. You set
> the tactics and spend command points; you do not get to choose who the enemy
> hits.
>
> It is difficult. That is the design, not an accident.

## What Makes It Different

Most management games model buildings and supply chains. Hero's Guild models
**people**, in more depth than the genre usually attempts:

- **Needs, mood and thoughts.** Energy, social, recreation and comfort all decay.
  Heroes accumulate individual thoughts with sources and expiry dates, and you
  can read exactly why a hero is unhappy
- **Three drifting value axes**, held by the guild *and* by every hero
  independently. When they diverge, the heroes have opinions about it
- **Bonds with teeth.** Some block a deployment outright; some are soft and can
  be overruled at a price in mood, paid up front
- **Hobbies and clubs.** Heroes take up pastimes, rank up in them, get rusty
  through neglect, found clubs, chair them, admit or turn away applicants, feud
  with rival clubs, and go on tour — occasionally not coming back
- **Injury that persists.** Body parts can be damaged, lost, or replaced with
  prosthetics. A hero who loses a hand may give up the hobby that needed two
- **Permadeath, and grief scaled to the relationship.** The empty chair in the
  key art above is a real mechanic
- **Player-made dungeons.** Build and publish your own; every submission must be
  cleared by its author first and carries a proof bound to the dungeon's layout

Every system above is fully documented in the public
[player guide](../) — twenty-one guides, written and kept current against the
build.

## Features

- **6 hero classes** — Warrior, Mage, Rogue, Ranger, Necromancer, Cleric
- **Ascendancy paths** — 2–3 specialisations per class, some of which remove
  abilities on purpose
- **Deep combat** — turn-based, resolving on initiative and threat; you set the
  tactics and spend command points, the heroes decide the rest
- **Relationships** — bonds, rivalries, romances, mental breaks and recovery
- **Quarters** — private rooms, decorations, cohabitation, and neighbours who
  matter
- **Hobbies and clubs** — a social economy that costs real gold
- **Four crafting disciplines** — Metalsmithing, Softcraft, Alchemy, Arcana
- **Procedural dungeons** — forests, crypts, volcanoes and worse
- **Endgame** — Heroic Dungeons, the Abyssal Spire, and World Boss raids with up
  to fifteen heroes on a tactical board
- **Custom dungeons** — design, validate and publish your own
