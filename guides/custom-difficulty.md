# Custom Difficulty — Sandbox Mode

Hero's Guild is difficult on purpose, and the Guild Clerk has filed a great many letters on the subject. Sandbox Mode is the answer to those letters: twenty-one dials, arranged in five groups, each one attached to a number the game was already using. Turn them up if the realm has been unkind. Turn them down if it has been soft. Nobody is going to stop you.

What nobody will do either is let you take it back.

## What It Costs

A sandbox guild plays off the record. Specifically:

- **No leaderboards.** Nothing is submitted and nothing is ranked. The leaderboard screen says so rather than leaving you wondering where your entry went
- **No achievements.** None of them fire, however deserved
- **No play statistics.** The guild sends nothing to the server about what it has been doing
- **No raid runs posted**, so a world-boss victory in a sandbox guild is a private triumph
- **No coming back.** A sandbox guild can never be a regular guild again. Not by turning everything back to its default, not by reloading, not by asking nicely

That last point is the whole of the fine print, and it is worth reading twice. The dials move freely for the rest of the guild's life; the decision to have dials at all is made exactly once.

## Turning It On

Settings grows a second tab while a game is loaded: **⚙ General** and **⚖ Sandbox**. The Sandbox tab shows all twenty-one dials from the start, greyed out and sitting at their defaults, so you can read what you would be getting before you get it.

To take them: tick **Enable sandbox mode**, read the warning, press **Confirm**. From that moment the dials are live and every change takes effect **at once** — no saving, no restart, no waiting for the day to turn. The panel notes the day you crossed over, in the manner of a tattoo.

Each dial is independent. There are no presets, no difficulty names, and nothing that moves two dials at the same time. If you want a guild where heroes never get ill but the money is thin, that is two dials and nobody's business but yours.

## Illness

| Dial | Default | Range | What it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Illness onset** | 100% | 0–200% | How often a hero falls ill without catching it from anyone — at home, from wounds, from bites, in a plague. At 0% they still catch things from each other; they simply stop inventing their own |
| **Illness spread** | 100% | 0–200% | How easily a sick hero passes it on |
| **Recovery speed** | 100% | 50–300% | How fast heroes build immunity and get well |

Illness is the system players write in about most, and it is also the one where turning a dial down changes the *texture* of the guild rather than just its arithmetic: a guild that never coughs has no Infirmary politics, no quarantine decisions and no mornings where half the roster is unavailable. That may be exactly what you wanted. It is worth knowing what you are trading.

## Relationships

| Dial | Default | Range | What it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Good interactions** | 100% | 50–200% | The share of daily meetings between heroes that go well |
| **Quarrels** | 100% | 0–200% | The share that go badly — small slights included — plus arguments and fights at the Tavern, and what an ignored quarrel costs you |
| **Grudge spread** | 100% | 0–200% | How strongly heroes turn against someone over what was done to a friend, or for helping an enemy |
| **Goodwill spread** | 100% | 0–200% | How warmly heroes take to someone who helped a friend, or wronged an enemy |

The first two share a ceiling. Good meetings and bad meetings are two slices of the same day, so if raising both would push the total past 100% of the day's interactions, the game shrinks them in proportion rather than inventing extra afternoons. At 0% quarrels nobody falls out with anybody, which produces a guild of relentless pleasantness that some players find restful and others find unnerving.

## Combat

| Dial | Default | Range | What it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hero damage** | 100% | 50–300% | The damage of every hero hit and skill. Counter-attacks, thorns, reflected damage, minion explosions and gem damage over time are **not** included |
| **Hero health** | 100% | 50–300% | The HP each point of VIT gives |
| **Death save** | +0 | −20 to +30 | Points added straight onto a fallen hero's chance of surviving |
| **Death save cap** | 95% | 50–100% | The highest chance a fallen hero can have. At **100%** the best-protected never die at all |
| **Injury roll** | +0 | −20 to +40 | Points added to the injury a hero takes after falling and surviving: more means milder injuries, fewer days off and less lasting damage |

**Injury roll does a second job.** It also lowers the chance of a wound *during* a fight — a knock-out, or a single hit past half a hero's health. Each **+10** points takes about a sixth off that chance, and each **−10** adds a sixth. So a guild that wants bruises rather than amputations moves one dial and gets both.

**The two death-save dials are not the same dial.** *Death save* raises the odds; *Death save cap* raises the ceiling those odds are allowed to reach. A hero with every protection in the game stacked on them is held at the cap, so lifting the points without lifting the cap eventually buys nothing. See [Death Saves](heroes.md#death-saves) for what the base chance is made of.

## Economy

| Dial | Default | Range | What it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reward gold** | 100% | 50–300% | Gold from contracts, dungeons, raids, the tower, quest chains and duplicate recipe scrolls. **Sales are not affected** |
| **Reward materials** | 100% | 50–300% | Materials, crafting currencies and raid tokens from those same rewards. **Purchases and salvage are not affected** |
| **Reputation** | 100% | 50–300% | Reputation the guild gains. **Losses are not affected**, the realm's memory for failure being exempt from adjustment |
| **Experience** | 100% | 50–300% | The experience heroes earn |
| **Hero wages** | 100% | 0–200% | What heroes are paid each day — and it is also their own money, so less pay means slower [dreams](heroes.md#the-dream) and fewer days off they can afford |
| **Level curve** | 2.5 | 2.0–3.0 | How steeply each level costs more than the last. Paragon points follow it |

**Scaled counts are rolled, not rounded.** A quantity that comes out at 1.3 pays **1**, or **2** three times in ten — so a setting between whole numbers averages out over a campaign instead of vanishing into a floor. A material reward never drops below **1** on a lower setting; the realm will stiff you, but it will not send a party home with literally nothing.

**Hero wages is the dial with two edges.** It is tempting to read it as a pure discount on the payroll, and for the first week it is. Then the heroes who are saving for a dream stop reaching it, the heroes who wanted a day off cannot buy one, and the mood figures start explaining themselves. Cheap heroes are cheap for a reason.

## Leaving the Guild

| Dial | Default | Range | What it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dream retirement** | On | On / Off | **On**: a hero whose dream is to leave buys it, throws a party and goes. **Off**: they buy it like any other dream and stay |
| **Desertion** | 100% | 0–200% | How often a hero at the end of their tether walks out for good instead of breaking some other way |
| **Crisis deaths** | On | On / Off | **On**: when a crisis is ignored, the unhappiest hero dies. **Off**: the crisis still costs gold, reputation and mood, but nobody dies |

The two toggles are the bluntest instruments in the panel. **Dream retirement off** keeps the hero who has spent sixty days saving for a cottage; they get the cottage, they are delighted about it, and they turn up for work on Monday. **Crisis deaths off** removes the single harshest consequence in the game while leaving the crisis itself fully intact — which, if you have lost a level-70 Cleric to a form you did not read in time, may be the dial you came here for.

## What Sandbox Mode Does Not Change

Every dial scales a number the game already had. None of them changes a rule.

Heroes still form the same bonds by the same arithmetic, hazards still work the way they work, the death save is still rolled at the same moment in the same pipeline, and a contract still fails for the same reasons. A sandbox guild at the defaults is playing the game exactly as written, number for number — which is why the panel can show you all twenty-one before you commit to any of them.

There are no new systems here and nothing is unlocked. This is a set of dials, not a cheat menu, and the Guild Clerk would be grateful if the difference were respected in correspondence.

## The One Real Exception

One thing *is* a rule rather than a number, and it exists so that the **Death save cap** means what it says.

In a regular guild, a destroyed vital organ is the end. The heart or the liver gone, or both of a paired set, and the hero is dead — no save, no second chance, no spare. In a **sandbox** guild that wound goes to the death save like any other: a hero who passes it survives, and the organ that killed them is left **critically damaged** instead of destroyed. It is still there. It has simply stopped speaking to the rest of the hero.

That is the only place where a sandbox guild plays by a different rule rather than a different figure, and it is the reason a cap of 100% can honestly promise that the best-protected never die. Without it, the promise would have an organ-shaped hole in it.
