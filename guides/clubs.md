# Hobbies and Clubs

Heroes do not spend every waking hour being heroic. Given an afternoon with nothing in it, a hero will discover knitting, and having discovered knitting will want somewhere to do it, and having found somewhere will want the guild to pay for the wool. This is how clubs happen. You are not obliged to say yes — but a guild where nobody has anything to do but wait for the next contract is a guild full of heroes thinking about the last one.

The whole business lives behind the **♣ Clubs** button in **Facilities**, beside Quarters, the Market and the Infirmary — which is the correct filing, a club being a room the guild pays for rather than an errand. It is there from the first day and it is empty until somebody asks you for something, which is the most honest page in the game.

## Hobbies

A hobby is not assigned. It is **discovered**, on a quiet day, by a hero who had a gap in the afternoon. The base chance is **2% a day**, multiplied by whatever made them restless:

| The hero is | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Resting | ×1.5 |
| In the Infirmary | ×3 |
| Drying out in detox | ×4 |
| Watching a friend enjoy their club | ×3 |

These multiply together, so a resting hero sits at about **3%** and a hero convalescing with a friend in a club is a good deal more likely to come out of it with an interest. Boredom and bed rest are the two great engines of the amateur arts.

**Which** hobby they find is weighted towards the ones they would be good at — the hobby leans on a stat, and a hero whose profile favours that stat is drawn to it. A hobby they once gave up can come back, at reduced odds. A good friend's club weighs its own hobby up **six times**, which is how clubs recruit without anybody holding a meeting about recruitment.

**Keenness** is rolled on discovery and is the hero's honest opinion of the thing:

| Keenness | Chance |
|---|---|
| Curious | 50% |
| Likes it | 35% |
| Loves it | 15% |

A hero keeps at most **four** hobbies at a time. A fifth displaces the one they were only *Curious* about and least far along — and if everything they have is loved, the new interest simply never takes hold. There is a limit to how many things one person can be enthusiastic about, and the game knows it.

### Ranks, hours and aptitude

Practice is counted in **hours**, and rank is a threshold on hours:

| Rank | Hours |
|---|---|
| Beginner | 0 |
| Keen | 40 |
| Devoted | 200 |
| Master | 800 |

Those hours are then scaled by **aptitude**, which comes from the hero's stat *profile* rather than their level — so a level 80 Mage still knits like a Mage. A hero built for the hobby needs as little as **×0.7** of the hours; one built against it as much as **×1.5**. Every rank gained also nudges keenness up one step, because competence is a gateway drug.

### Rust

A hobby left alone for **30 days** starts to slip: **half an hour a day** drains away, and the first slip costs **−2 mood** and a line in the hero's chronicle about the floor having moved further away in their absence. Rust has a floor, though — it will never drag a hero below the start of the rank *before* the best one they ever reached. A Master who takes a year off is a rusty Devoted, not a beginner. Some things you do not entirely lose.

**Giving up** outright is rare: **0.04% a day**, tripled for a merely Curious hero and cut to under a third for one who loves it. The exception is brutal and deliberate — a hero who has **lost a hand** faces a **5% daily chance** of abandoning any hobby that needs two of them. Pottery, woodcarving, knitting, ships in bottles, an instrument and fitboxing all need two hands. This is the only place in the guild where an amputation shows up as a line in a chronicle about a kiln.

## What the Heroes Take Up

The daily fee below is what a **guild-funded** club costs at Beginner rank; the rank multiplier in [Founding a Club](#founding-a-club) raises it from there. "The neighbours get" is what the rest of the guild has to put up with on a club night — covered properly under [The Neighbours](#the-neighbours).

| Hobby | Leans on | Fee / day | The neighbours get |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilates | Vitality | 240 | Very little |
| Yoga | Vitality | 120 | Very little |
| Tai chi | Vitality | 120 | Very little |
| Fitboxing | Strength | 240 | Thuds |
| Fishing | Luck | 120 | A smell |
| Hunting | Dexterity | 240 | A smell |
| Gardening | Vitality | 240 | **Produce** |
| Pottery | Dexterity | 370 | **Gifts** |
| Woodcarving | Dexterity | 240 | **Gifts** |
| Knitting | Dexterity | 120 | **Scarves** |
| Ships in bottles | Dexterity | 240 | Very little |
| Painting | Intelligence | 240 | Very little |
| An instrument | Dexterity | 370 | Sound |
| Singing | Vitality | 120 | Sound |
| Battle reenactment | Strength | 370 | Noise |
| Chess | Intelligence | 120 | Very little |
| Dice and dragons | Intelligence | 120 | Cheering |

Four of those are worth the guild's attention. **Gardening** and **knitting** make their neighbours *happier* the better the club gets — vegetables and scarves respectively — and **pottery** and **woodcarving** turn into gifts. A Master choir is also, eventually, a pleasure to live under. A Master reenactment society never is.

## Founding a Club

You do not found clubs. A hero asks, and you answer, on the guild events screen.

No hero asks before **day 10** — the same floor the guild's own events wait for, on the grounds that a request for funding in the first week is an imposition. After that, the daily chance that a hero asks for a club of a hobby that has none depends on how far into it they are:

| Their rank | Daily chance |
|---|---|
| Beginner | 1% |
| Keen | 10% |
| Devoted | 20% |
| Master | 30% |

Having asked about anything, that hero asks about **nothing** for **60 days**. A hero whose request you refuse waits **60 days** before raising that hobby again — and is told the number, which they remember with the precision people reserve for grievances.

### What you can say

- **Fund it.** The guild pays. **+6 mood** to the founder, a room in the Club Wing if one is free, and a founding charge of **20 days of the Beginner fee** up front.
- **Refuse.** **−3 mood for a week.** A *second* refusal of the same club earns a **−4 mood grudge for 30 days** and leaves the club itself sore with the guild, which matters later.
- **Promise.** You agree to fund it *later* — **after the season** (30 days), **next year** (100 days) or **one day, certainly** (200 days). The hero is **+3 mood and hopeful for the entire wait**, which is the cheapest morale in the game and also a debt. Keeping the promise is **+9**. Breaking it is **−9 for 45 days**, and they will not forget who said what.

A refused hero may found the club **themselves**. A **self-funded** club costs the guild nothing, meets in the founder's own quarters, and holds **four** people. It is also a standing reminder that they asked and you said no. You can **adopt** one later, for **+4 mood** and a considerable easing of the grudge.

### What it costs to keep

The daily fee is the hobby's fee multiplied by the **club's** rank — a good choir costs more than a bad one, because it has opinions about its piano:

| Club rank | Fee multiplier |
|---|---|
| Beginner | ×1 |
| Keen | ×1.5 |
| Devoted | ×2.5 |
| Master | ×4 |

That lands in your daily expenses as its own **Clubs** line, alongside wages and facility upkeep. Funding a Master reenactment society is a four-figure daily decision. If you stop paying, the members cover it themselves and sit at **−3 mood** for as long as they do, which is a slow leak rather than a crisis, and will be mentioned.

A hero belongs to at most **two** clubs at once. A hero sitting on a **waiting list** who is already in two is passed over when a place opens, and **keeps their place in the queue** — they get in once they have left one of the others. The list does not punish ambition; it merely declines to reward it twice. The dead and the departed are struck off as they are reached, which is the only paperwork in the building that does itself.

## The Club Wing

The wing is built a **floor at a time**, at the same prices as Hero Quarters floors:

| Floor | Cost |
|---|---|
| Ground Floor | 50,000 |
| Second Floor | 150,000 |
| Third Floor | 400,000 |
| Fourth Floor | 800,000 |
| Fifth Floor | 1,500,000 |
| Sixth Floor | 3,000,000 |

Every floor has the **same five rooms** off one corridor, and each room is the size it is — nobody sells walls by the yard:

| Room | Holds | Where |
|---|---|---|
| The Broom Cupboard | 3 | By the stair |
| The Smoking Room | 4 | Off the corridor |
| The Old Armoury | 6 | Off the corridor |
| The Map Room | 8 | By the stair |
| The Refectory | 12 | Off the corridor |

Two things follow from that plan. The **Broom Cupboard and the Map Room sit by the stair**, and what happens in them goes down it to the sleeping quarters at **×1.5** nuisance. And rooms that **share a wall** — Cupboard with Smoking Room, Smoking Room with Old Armoury, Map Room with Refectory — hear each other, which clubs take personally. The corridor keeps the two sides apart; floors do not hear each other at all.

**Twelve is the ceiling.** A club that outgrows the Refectory keeps everyone it has but can take nobody new.

### Furnishing the room

Each room upgrades through tiers, exactly as Quarters do:

| Tier | Cost | Upkeep / day | Hours | Mood | Soundproofing | Equipment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Back Pantry | — | 30 | ×1 | +0 | none | 1 |
| The Snug | 10,000 | 80 | ×1.1 | +1 | 20% | 2 |
| The Club Room | 50,000 | 200 | ×1.2 | +1 | 40% | 3 |
| The Long Gallery | 200,000 | 500 | ×1.35 | +2 | 60% | 4 |
| The Grand Salon | 500,000 | 1,000 | ×1.5 | +3 | 80% | 5 |

Equipment fills those slots: a **matching** piece — a kiln for the potters, a piano for the choir, mirrors for the yoga — adds **5% to the hours**, and a piece that suits anybody, like a trophy case, adds **+1 mood** instead. Costs run from 2,000 for boards and clocks to 12,000 for a piano that is slightly out of tune, which gives everyone an excuse.

A night is **crowded** above **75%** of the room's capacity: **−1 mood per attendee** over the line. A thriving club in the Broom Cupboard is a worse evening than a small one in the Refectory.

**Moving a club** always grumbles — **−2 mood**, and **−4** if the new room is worse, against **+3** if it is better. Moving one into a room too small turns out its **newest** members, never the chair, at **−4 mood** each and back onto the waiting list.

## Club Nights

Each club meets on fixed **weekdays**, named when it is founded. On the night, every member turns up unless they are away, or tired, or sulking: a hero below **25 energy** or **30 mood** has a **50%** chance of sending apologies, which the minutes record by name and reason.

What a member gets out of the evening:

- **2.5 hours** of practice, multiplied by the room's bonus and any matching equipment
- **+15 recreation**, on top of the day's pastime
- **+3 mood** before company, plus the room's mood bonus
- **+1 relationship** with everybody else who came

Then the **company** is counted, one score per other attendee, by how the hero feels about them:

| The other member is | Points |
|---|---|
| A close friend | +2 |
| A friend | +1 |
| Disliked | −1.5 |
| An enemy | −4 |

That total is capped at **±6** and converted at **1 mood per point**. The arithmetic is unsentimental: a single enemy across the table outweighs two friends beside you, and an evening that lands at **−1 or below** was **sour** for that hero. This is the whole reason the chair's opinion of applicants matters, and it is covered under [The Chair](#the-chair).

**Leaving.** The club counts the whole evening as sour when **half or more** of those who came had a sour one, and keeps a running tally of consecutive sour nights — one decent evening resets it to zero. Every member's daily chance of walking out is then **0.1%**, plus **1% for each night in that streak**, plus **1%** if they share the club with an enemy. At three in a row the minutes stop being diplomatic and start recording that somebody left “after one sour evening too many”.

Note that the streak is the *club's*, not the individual's: a sour run puts every member at the door, including the ones who had a perfectly nice time. Clubs rarely collapse from lack of funding. They collapse because two members cannot stand each other and nobody did anything about it.

**Club Mates.** Members who attend **12 nights** together while on Friendly terms or better form the **Club Mates** bond, worth **+5 mood** to both for as long as it lasts. It is the only bond in the guild that can be manufactured on purpose, with a budget, by furnishing a room.

### The daily session

A member whose ordinary daily hobby roll lands on their club's hobby goes and practises **in the club room** rather than alone: **1 hour**, **+15 recreation**, and no charge, since the guild is paying for the room anyway. It disturbs the neighbours at **half** a night's strength. The room you funded therefore earns its keep on days when nothing is scheduled, which is the nearest thing to a bargain in this guide.

## The Neighbours

Everybody not in the club gets an opinion about it. How bad tonight was depends on the club's **rank**, how many came — a **full house is four or more** — and a roll between **×0.5 and ×1.5**:

| What they inflict | Beginner | Keen | Devoted | Master |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sound (singing, an instrument) | −4 | −2 | −0.5 | **+1.5** |
| Noise (reenactment) | −2.5 | −2 | −1.5 | −1 |
| Cheering (dice and dragons) | −3 | −2 | −1.5 | −1 |
| Thuds (fitboxing) | −2 | −1.5 | −1 | −1 |
| A smell (fishing, hunting) | −1.5 | −1.5 | −1.5 | −1.5 |
| Gifts (pottery, woodcarving) | −1 | −0.5 | **+0.5** | **+1** |
| Scarves (knitting) | −0.5 | 0 | **+0.5** | **+1** |
| Produce (gardening) | **+0.5** | **+1** | **+1.5** | **+2** |
| Very little | −0.5 | −0.3 | 0 | 0 |

A choir improves with practice, and so, in time, does everyone's opinion of it. A fishing club never will — the smell does not get better at fishing.

**Soundproofing** keeps in sound, noise, smells, thuds and cheering. It does nothing whatever about gifts, scarves and vegetables, which find you anyway.

Then each listener scales it themselves. Abrasive, Pessimist, Misanthrope, Volatile, Paranoid, Loner, Reserved and Ascetic heroes mind **40% more**; Cheerful, Forgiving, Kind, Gregarious, Approachable and Peacemaker heroes mind **40% less**. A hero below **40 mood** minds **30% more**, a bored one **20% more**, and a music lover feels a choir — good or bad — **half as much again**. A **friend** among the members **halves** it; an **enemy** sharpens it by **half**, and a member of a club they are feuding with counts double.

### Complaints

When it gets bad enough, the complaint arrives on the events screen with four answers:

- **Move the club** to another room
- **Pay for the walls** — upgrade the room's soundproofing
- **Ask them to keep it down** — **half** nuisance for **14 days**, and **−3 mood** to every member, who sulk
- **Do nothing**, and let the complainant go on minding

## Clubs and Each Other

Two clubs in the same building will, given time, find something to disagree about. Each pair holds a score:

| Tier | Score |
|---|---|
| Allied | +50 and above |
| Friendly | +20 to +49 |
| Neutral | −30 to +19 |
| Rival | −70 to −31 |
| Feud | below −70 |

Every week the score **drifts 15% of the way** towards how the two memberships actually feel about each other, so club politics is downstream of hero politics, eventually. Shared members warm it by **+1** a week. What moves it faster:

- **Poaching** — **−6**, when a hero joins one club within **14 days** of leaving another
- **Sabotage** — a **15%** weekly chance between clubs already sore with each other: **−6** and **−2 mood**, and a note in the minutes about the trophy case having been rearranged, insultingly
- **Room envy** — **−1** a week while one club sits **two tiers** above the other
- **Noise through a shared wall** — **−1**
- **A snub** — **−1**
- **A joint evening** — a **10%** weekly chance between friendly clubs: **+3** and **+2 mood**, chaotic, loud and by general agreement the best night of the year
- **Being somebody's ally** — **−2** a week, dragged into a partner's feuds, which is what allies are for

**Nobody may belong to two rival clubs.** When a pair crosses into Rival, any shared member must choose, at **−3 mood**. Making up takes a little more than scraping back over the line — the score has to climb past **−20** before the two clubs will have each other in the room again.

## The Chair

The founder chairs the club, and the chair decides **who gets in**. Each hero who asks is accepted or turned away according to how the chair feels about them:

| The chair thinks they are | Admitted |
|---|---|
| A friend | Always |
| Friendly | 90% |
| Neutral | 80% |
| Annoying | 60% |
| Disliked | 40% |
| An enemy | 5% |

A welcoming chair lets in **15%** more; a sour one that much fewer. Being turned away costs the applicant **−3 mood** and **−5** on their opinion of the chair, and they will not try that club again for **30 days**.

This is a real lever and it points both ways. A chair who dislikes half the guild runs a small, harmonious club. A popular chair runs a full one that may well contain two people who loathe each other, and will find out about it on the night.

The chair may also **name a successor** from the membership — worth doing, because the alternative to a named successor is whatever the club decides on its own, at the worst possible moment. Heroes a chair has turned away are remembered, along with the day they may ask again.

## Days Out

**Seminars.** You can buy in a teacher. A **local** one costs **5 days** of the club's fee and gives **4 hours**; a **renowned** one costs **25 days** and gives **10 hours**. The renowned sort also comes with a considerably higher chance of clashing with the members, being intimidating, being a fraud, or charming somebody entirely — a 25% chance of a personality clash is the price of expertise.

**Invitations.** A guild-funded club that has got good enough starts getting asked out. The guild is **paid** for this, in days of the club's fee, and gains **reputation**:

| Invitation | From rank | Weekly | Away | Members | Paid | Reputation | Goes badly |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Local — a harvest fair, a wedding, the Lord Mayor's birthday | Keen | 8% | An evening | Any | 3 days | +2 | 5% |
| City — the opera, the river championship, the Spring Exhibition | Devoted | 4% | 1–2 days | Up to 6 | 10 days | +6 | 20% |
| A tour — the southern cities, a season on the coast | Master | 2% | 7–10 days | Up to 4 | 30 days | +15 | 10% |

Glory is **+6 mood**; humiliation is **−4** and costs reputation instead of earning it. **Declining** an invitation is **−3 mood** and a grudge. And the tour has a tail you should read twice: **15%** per member of coming back *changed*, and **5%** of **not coming back at all**. A hero can walk out of your guild because the coast circuit was more interesting than you were.

A **rival** club good enough to have been asked, and not asked, minds at **−2**.

**The Master's big day.** A Master has a **3%** weekly chance of asking for their hobby's great occasion — two days away, entirely for themselves. Letting them go is **+6 mood**. Refusing is **−5**. It is two days of one hero's time against the difference between those two numbers, and the arithmetic is rarely in your favour.
